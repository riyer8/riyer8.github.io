## Play Without Permission 🎈

In TAPS 103, we return to a childlike mindset. This isn’t about being “childish,” but rather letting go of the self-critic that often censors our thoughts and movements before we create an offer. Through play, we embrace flexibility and spontaneity in dialogue and movement. This mindset also turns ordinary offers into opportunities for exploration, curiosity, and wonder, treating each idea as if it’s being seen for the first time.

By learning to drop the inner self-critic, our personalities and imperfections are brought to the surface. We learn that there is no use in stockpiling during games or scenes, but rather pay intentional focus on the actions in front of us.

> stockpiling _(noun)_: the practice of quietly gathering and storing ideas, lines, or actions for later use, often to heighten comedic payoff.

We don't need to wait for permission to play. In fact, we are expected to bring our ideas to the table - whether the offer is taken or not.
