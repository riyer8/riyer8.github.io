## Make Your Partner Look Good 🤝

The idea that we should make our partner look good is best seen in the well-known principle of "Yes, And". [Clay Drinko](https://medium.com/better-humans/how-improv-thinking-helped-me-get-out-of-my-head-and-into-the-moment-a2bff6401548) explains this really well.

> The most famous improv principle is “Yes, And” — also known as the rule of agreement. The idea is that you need to agree with whatever reality your scene partner is trying to create and then add new details to that same reality.

With the principle of "Yes, And", we collaborate with our partner to make a faithful attempt at the reality that we jointly bring to the table. With this principle, we garner a level of trust with our partner that we won't get sabotaged in the moment, allowing creativity to take over instead of self-doubt.

One of the cool ideas we learned is to **have a reason to leave.** A form of improv is done by stitching together snapshot scenes. Every scene must have a reason to leave, but within the scene, the audience should feel a progression - a sense that a relationship or emotion has changed.

When you make it your mission to make your partner look good, you don't feel the need to overthink (with the hopes that they have a similar mission). It doesn't allow you to be self-critical and also removes you from being self-centered.

Allow your partner to endow you with the characteristics about your character and embrace it. Endow your partner with characteristics that brighten them up too.
