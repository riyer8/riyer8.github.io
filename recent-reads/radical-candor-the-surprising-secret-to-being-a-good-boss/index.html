Radical Candor — The Surprising Secret to Being a Good Boss

from this entry

HHIPP: “Radical candor is humble, it’s helpful, it’s immediate, it’s in person — in private if it’s criticism and in public if it’s praise — and it doesn’t personalize.
TL;DR
Radical Candor is the intersection of personally caring for someone and challenging them directly, allowing for the most direct feedback.
Thoughts
A really interesting way to think about providing (and receiving) feedback.
Notes

This article was actually recommended during my CS 183E class at Stanford about effective leadership. I think the frameworks addressed here are incredibly important, especially to enhance a culture of providing helpful feedback.

The single most important thing a boss can do, Scott has learned, is focus on guidance: giving it, receiving it, and encouraging it.

Scott goes on to describe that guidance is a synonym for feedback, but often feedback carries a negative connotation that may not always be well perceived.

I would argue that criticizing your employees when they screw up is not just your job, it's actually your moral obligation.
